To pack fragile items safely for a house move, wrap each piece individually in packing paper or bubble wrap, cushion boxes with scrunched paper at the bottom and top, pack plates vertically like records rather than stacked flat, fill all gaps so nothing shifts during loading and unloading, and label every box “FRAGILE” with the destination room. Glass, electronics, mirrors and artwork need extra layers and rigid outer cartons. Professional packers use specialist packing materials and techniques to protect goods in transit.

## Essential packing materials for fragile items

Before you start, gather the right supplies. Cheap or reused boxes that have lost rigidity are a common cause of breakages on domestic removals across Wigan and Greater Manchester.

### What you need

- **Sturdy double-walled boxes** in small and medium sizes. Heavy fragile items belong in smaller boxes.
- **Bubble wrap** for glasses, ornaments and electronics
- **Packing paper** (unprinted newsprint) for wrapping and filling gaps
- **Foam pouches** for plates and bowls where available
- **Packing tape** applied generously along box seams
- **Marker pens** for labelling on more than one side
- **Corner protectors and cardboard sheets** for mirrors, artwork and television screens
- **Stretch wrap** to secure drawers and loose parts on furniture

## How to pack glassware and china

### Glasses and stemware

Stuff each glass with crumpled packing paper, then wrap the outside. Stemware needs extra paper around the stem. Stand glasses upright in the box, never on their sides. Use cell dividers if you have them. Fill every gap so nothing can rattle when the box is lifted.

### Plates, bowls and serving dishes

Wrap each plate individually. Pack plates **on their edge**, like vinyl records, with paper between each one. A stack of plates packed flat is more likely to crack under its own weight during loading and unloading. Bowls can nest if each is fully wrapped. Put the heaviest pieces at the bottom of a small box.

### China sets and ornaments

Keep sets together in one clearly labelled carton so you are not hunting through the kitchen at the new house. Figurines and odd-shaped ornaments need bubble wrap plus a paper nest. Do not pack china in supermarket banana boxes: single-wall cardboard collapses when stacked in a Luton van.

## How to pack electronics

Televisions, monitors, games consoles and hi-fi equipment are among the most claimed items on domestic removals when packing is rushed.

### Screens and televisions

If you still have the original box and foam inserts, use them. Otherwise, protect the screen with a cardboard sheet or foam, wrap the set in blankets or stretch wrap, and keep it upright during loading and unloading. Never lay a large television flat on a pile of boxes. Photograph cable connections before you unplug anything.

### Computers and small electronics

Back up laptops and PCs before packing. Remove printer ink cartridges and pack them separately in a sealed bag. Wrap each device, coil cables, and bag them with a label so they stay with the correct item. Keep hard drives and backup disks with you rather than on the van if they hold irreplaceable files.

## Mirrors, artwork and picture frames

Tape a large X of masking tape across glass on framed pictures to reduce shatter spread if the glass cracks. Corner protectors matter more than people think: most frame damage happens at the corners during loading. Wrap in paper, then bubble wrap, then a rigid cardboard sandwich. Mark “GLASS” and “THIS WAY UP”. Large mirrors often travel better as a dedicated, upright item strapped inside the van rather than buried in a mixed carton.

## Labelling and van placement

Every fragile box should be marked on **all sides** with:

- “FRAGILE”
- “THIS WAY UP” arrows where orientation matters
- Destination room (for example Kitchen, Lounge, Bedroom 1)

During loading and unloading, fragile cartons should sit on top of heavier boxes, be secured with straps, and stay away from items that could puncture them.
