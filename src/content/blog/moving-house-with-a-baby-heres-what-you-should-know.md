A baby does not care that completion is at midday or that the Luton is booked for 8am. They care about milk, sleep and a familiar adult. Moving house with a baby in Wigan is less about a perfect timetable and more about protecting those three things while someone else handles packing, loading and unloading of the furniture.

You cannot make moving day calm. You can stop it becoming unsafe or impossible. This is what actually helps.

## Build a schedule, then assume it will slip

Write a list in priority order: survey booked, packing of non-baby rooms, childcare for the day, first-night nursery kit, solicitor or landlord times. If a feed overruns or a nap collapses, you drop the lowest item, not the baby’s routine.

## Get help for the noisy hours

The highest-risk window is loading and unloading: open doors, trailing blankets, banisters used as climbing frames, kettles on the floor. A baby on the hip while you direct a three-piece suite is how people get hurt.

The best trick is simple: another adult whose only job is the child, off-site if possible. Grandparents, a friend, or a paid sitter for the core hours. If the baby must stay, set up one closed room with the travel cot, nappies and a sign on the door, and tell the crew that room is out of bounds until the van is shut.

Do not “just pop them in the front room” while boxes move through it.

## Pack an essentials bag you can grab in the dark

This bag stays with you, not on the van:

- Nappies, wipes, nappy sacks, changing mat
- Formula or breastfeeding kit, sterilising gear if you use it, bottles
- Two changes of baby clothes and one for you
- Favourite sleep aid: muslin, white-noise machine, dummy
- Medication, thermometer, teething gel
- Snacks and water for adults
- Portable blackout or a thick muslin for an unfamiliar window

If you cannot find this bag in ten seconds, it is packed wrong. The same rule as jewellery and documents: they travel in the car.

## Set the nursery up first, not last

At the new house, the crew should place the cot, changing unit and a chest of drawers before they start on your wardrobe. Say so at the walkthrough. If dismantling and reassembly of the cot was quoted, get it built and checked (all bolts, correct mattress height) before anyone leaves.

Make the cot, plug in the monitor, put a lamp where you can find it at 2am. Then unpack adult beds. A baby who can sleep lets you finish the kitchen tomorrow. A baby who cannot sleep means nobody unpacks anything well.

If you are moving within [Wigan’s neighbourhoods](/areas/), try to keep the first night visually familiar: same bedding, same mobile, same white noise. The house can wait.

## Safety while the house is a building site

New properties hide risks that your old one had already been baby-proofed against: unboxed cleaning products, loose stair spindles, open garden gates, trailing wrap. Do a ten-minute hazard walk before the baby comes in. Fit socket covers later; hide chemicals now.

Keep one playpen or travel cot as a safe island while cartons occupy the floor. Do not let older siblings “help” on the ramp.

## Feeds, breaks and your own limits

Put feeds and naps on the plan as seriously as the solicitor’s call. Eat. Sit down. If the day is running long, it is better to pause unloading of non-essentials than to push through with a hysterical infant and two exhausted adults. Tell the foreman; a professional domestic removals team would rather a clear instruction than a silent meltdown.

Register with a local GP and health visitor after the move, and keep your red book in the essentials bag, not in a carton marked “office”.

## A baby-move checklist

- Survey and fixed quote booked
- Childcare for loading and unloading
- Essentials bag packed and with you
- Nursery furniture placed and built first
- Hazard walk before the baby enters
- First-night bedding and milk sorted
- Valuables off the van
- Permission to stop if the baby cannot cope
