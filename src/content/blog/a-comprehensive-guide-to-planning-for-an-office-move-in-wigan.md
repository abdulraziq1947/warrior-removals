An office move in Wigan is a project with a deadline, not a slightly larger house move. Staff still need to work, clients still need to reach you, and a dead broadband line on Monday morning is more expensive than a well-planned Saturday cutover. This guide covers the planning sequence that keeps commercial domestic-style removals, desks, chairs, IT, archives, on time without turning the week into chaos.

## Start with one owner and a real survey

Name a move coordinator who can make decisions. That person books the survey, holds the timeline, and is the only voice the crew takes instructions from on the day. Split authority is how desks end up on the wrong floor.

Book an in-person or video survey early. Commercial volume hides in pedestal drawers, archive rooms and “we might take the old meeting table”. A survey captures stairs versus lifts, loading bays, parking on streets such as Standishgate, and whether workstations need dismantling and reassembly.

Use the [moving cost calculator](/moving-cost-calculator/) for a first range. Budget against the written quote, not the range.

## Inventory before anyone reaches for tape

Photograph and list what is moving. Tag monitors to desks. Decide what will be sold or disposed of **separately**. We move office belongings to the new address; we do not run a clearance of broken chairs and bin bags.

Label cables. Back up servers and laptops. Pack confidential HR and finance files in sealed cartons, not open crates in a shared corridor. Personal plants, food and valuables go home with staff, they are not part of house removal services for the office.

## Sequence IT and broadband ahead of furniture

The physical move fails quietly if the new line is live on Wednesday and the desks arrive on Tuesday with nowhere to plug in. Agree a freeze on non-essential IT changes. Photograph rack layouts. Confirm who reconnects equipment: usually your IT provider, not the removal crew, unless that work is explicitly quoted.

If you need professional packing for screens, printers and the kitchen, schedule it the day before moving day so loading is a transport job, not a boxing job with the van on the clock.

## Tell staff exactly what “packed” means

Issue a one-page brief: date, what they pack, what stays until last thing Friday, and where personal items go. Provide packing materials for drawer contents. Ask people not to leave mugs of tea in pedestals.

A clear brief protects the quote. A floor of unpacked drawers is how commercial jobs overrun.

## Access in Wigan is a planning item, not a hope

Town-centre suites often need bay bookings, lift slots and landlord permission. Business parks around Leigh, Haydock and Ashton-in-Makerfield may have docks with timed windows. Restricted kerbs need a Wigan Council dispensation if the Luton must stand outside.

Share floor plans, codes and an on-the-day mobile number with us before move day. We work 7am-9pm, seven days a week, which suits firms that want an out-of-hours load to protect trading hours.

## Paperwork that sits beside the van

Change of address with Companies House, HMRC, banks and insurers. Update the website, email footers and Google Business Profile. Notify clients. Transfer utilities. Review dilapidations on the old lease. Refresh fire and health-and-safety documents for the new layout. None of that is the crew’s job, but forgetting it makes a “successful” move feel unfinished.

## Move day and the first week

Walk both sites with the foreman. Protect floors. Load systematically. Unload to a labelled floor plan. Rebuild desks if quoted. Then, before staff return: IT live, fire exits known, first-aid kit found, reception usable.

The relocation is finished when people can work, not when the tail lift goes up.
