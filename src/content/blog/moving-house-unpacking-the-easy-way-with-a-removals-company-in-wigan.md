The van pulling away is not the end of a house move. It is the start of the part people underestimate: living among cartons while you try to find the kettle, the cot sheets and the charger. Unpacking is easier when packing and loading were organised, and when your removal crew puts furniture and boxes in the right rooms instead of a single pile in the hall.

If you are using [house removal services in Wigan](/house-removals-wigan/), you can set unpacking up days before moving day. Here is a practical order that works in terraces, new-builds and family homes from Swinley out to Leigh.

## Unpacking starts with how you pack

Every box should show a **destination room** and a short contents note. “Kitchen, everyday crockery” beats “kitchen bits”. Fragile cartons need “FRAGILE” on more than one side so they stay on top during loading and unloading.

Pack a **first-night kit** that never goes deep into the van: kettle, mugs, tea, snacks, phone chargers, toilet roll, soap, a change of clothes, bedding for each bed you will sleep in, basic tools, and any medication. Carry documents and jewellery yourself.

If Warrior Removals is packing for you, we label by room as standard so unloading is already a sorting system. Self-packers who skip labels pay for it at 9pm with a Stanley knife and a dozen identical brown cubes.

## What the crew should do on arrival

A full domestic removal is not a man-and-van dump. Furniture should be placed in the rooms you name, beds and wardrobes rebuilt if dismantling was quoted, and boxes stacked by room with walkways left clear.

Walk the new property with the foreman before the tail lift goes down. Point to “master bedroom”, “kids’ room”, “office”. If you are moving within [Wigan’s local areas](/areas/), we will already have seen access at survey; still confirm which door you want used and which floors are out of bounds.

Stay until unloading finishes. Directing boxes for twenty minutes saves three evenings of swapping cartons between floors.

## Unpack in a living order, not a pretty order

Start with the rooms that let you function.

### 1. Sleeping

Beds first, then bedding from the first-night kit. A made bed means you can stop at a sensible hour even if the lounge is still boxed. If reassembly was booked, ask the crew to build beds before they leave.

### 2. Bathroom and kettle

Toiletries, towels, loo roll, then the kettle and mugs. You can live without picture frames. You cannot live without a working bathroom and a hot drink.

### 3. Children’s and baby spaces

If you have little ones, set up their room before your own décor. Familiar toys on the floor reduce the evening meltdown. Keep that room as a closed, safe space while the rest of the house is a building site.

### 4. Kitchen, enough to cook

Everyday pans, a chopping board, cutlery, the toaster. Leave the fancy serving dishes for the weekend. Check the cooker and stopcock locations while you are in there.

### 5. Everything else

Lounge, books, spare room, garage. Work one room to “done” rather than opening five boxes in five rooms. Flatten cartons as you go so you can see the floor.

## Make temporary homes for homeless items

Until pictures hang and shelves go up, use one designated “parking” zone per floor for things that have no home yet. That stops the dining table becoming a permanent dumping ground.

Keep a roll of bin bags for genuine rubbish, packing paper, broken hangers, and remember we are not a waste collection. Flattened boxes can go to the tip or be offered locally; they should not sit in the hall for a month.

## Use the crew for the heavy bit, you for the decisions

Professional loading and unloading protect furniture. Unpacking ornaments is usually quicker yourself, at your own pace, once the heavy pieces are where they belong. If you want packers to unpack kitchens or wardrobes, say so at quote stage so it is in the fixed price, not an awkward add-on on the drive.

Cover on the move is £50,000 goods in transit per vehicle load and £1 million public liability. Inspect for transit damage before the crew leaves. Photograph anything that concerns you. Signing off means you were happy with placement and visible condition.

## A 48-hour unpacking plan

**Moving day:** beds, bathroom, kettle, children’s room, fridge food.  
**Next morning:** kitchen everyday kit, school bags, work clothes, broadband router.  
**Next evening:** one full room to finished. Repeat.

That rhythm beats a heroic 2am unpack that leaves everyone exhausted for the school run.
